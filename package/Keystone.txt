3:T1190,Keystone by Seraphin Ernst – accurate Rhino 8 tools for architects. VectorView: vector drawings with exact shadows of the viewport you work in, exactly as it shows the model – one click, nothing to pick – isometric, elevation, plan or perspective, including two-point perspective, and sections: Rhino's clipping planes cut the model, the cut surfaces come out as filled section regions with heavy outlines (Rhino's section style colours are kept), cut open surfaces as section lines. The isometric is drawn true to length (DIN), so it measures correctly at scale; elevations and plans are true to scale; shadows are computed from Rhino's sun. Every visible face and every cast shadow becomes a closed, non-overlapping region with a solid hatch and its outline, exported to DXF (shadows truly transparent) or DWG at 1:1 in millimetres – shadows above the faces, or no shadows in the CAD file at all, and optionally the faces on one layer per Rhino layer (walls, roof, windows). One checkbox adds the curves of the model – lines, arcs, road markings – drawn where they are seen and exported with the rest. Commands: VectorView, VectorViewSettings, VectorViewPanel. TrussBuilder: trusses sized while you design. Plane trusses along any curve (Pratt, Howe, Warren, K, X) and steel space frames over a rectangle, with Eurocode-based preliminary design – steel EN 1993, timber EN 1995 – showing utilisation, deflection, mass and quantities in a live preview of the sized result. Preliminary design only; it does not replace structural verification by an engineer. Commands: TrussBuilder, TrussBuilderSpaceFrame, TrussBuilderPanel. BrickBuilder: brick walls laid as on site. Walls along any curve in real bonds – stretcher, raking stretcher, header, English, English cross, Flemish, Monk, Silesian, American, wild and stack bond – in NF, DF, 2DF, Waal, UK, US or custom formats, from half a brick up to four bricks thick with the bond carried through the whole wall. Corners and closers follow masonry rules; with whole bricks the head joints adjust to the wall length instead of cutting bricks. Windows and doors go into finished walls – with soldier courses, flat, segmental or round arches, precast or concrete lintels and rowlock sills. Course plans and a pieces list with order quantities. Commands: BrickBuilder, BrickBuilderOpening, BrickBuilderPanel. StairBuilder: stairs that pass the code. Draw the walking line, enter the storey height – straight, L and U stairs with landings, winder stairs, spiral and helical stairs, checked against DIN 18065 (also ASR A1.8, DIN 18040, OIB-RL 4, SIA 358, Approved Document K, IRC/IBC) with the calculation shown, built in steel, timber, reinforced concrete or stone with a preliminary design, or as plain geometry – also over several storeys, with floor landings at the exits, and in any width. Preliminary design only; it does not replace structural verification by an engineer. Commands: StairBuilder, StairBuilderEdit, StairBuilderPanel. RoofBuilder: roof coverings laid on your surfaces. Select plane or curved roof faces – RoofBuilder measures the pitch and suggests what suits it after the German roofing rules (Regeldachneigung) or SIA 232/1: plain tiles in double lap or crown covering with five cuts, interlocking and flat low-pitch tiles, pantiles, monk and nun, concrete tiles, natural slate (rectangular, scale, Old German with rising courses), fibre cement slates, corrugated sheets, wood and bitumen shingles, standing seam, trapezoidal and corrugated metal, metal diamonds, thatch, PV in-roof, flat roof membrane and green roof. The gauge is fitted to the slope, pieces are cut along verges, hips and valleys, with ridge and hip tiles, valley sheets, verge flashings, gutters, battens and counter battens (or the covering alone), a pitch check per face and the pieces to order. Gable, hip, mansard and L-shaped roofs, dormers, barrel vaults, domes and cones. Commands: RoofBuilder, RoofBuilderEdit, RoofBuilderPanel. Claude: every tool also works through Claude. One click in the Keystone panel ("Connect to Claude…", command KeystoneClaude) adds Keystone to the Claude desktop app – optionally together with McNeel's free Rhino MCP – then you describe the wall, truss, stair, roof or drawing and Claude builds it in your open model, each step one undo step.
